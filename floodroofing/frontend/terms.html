Measurements are estimates

This is the most important clause on the page. RoofMap helps you measure. It does not measure for you.

Everything RoofMap produces is an estimate, and you are responsible for checking it before you rely on it. That includes roof areas, line lengths, pitches, sheet and flashing cut lists, material quantities, labour hours and prices.

A measurement taken from an aerial photograph depends on the age and accuracy of that photograph, on the pitch you enter, and on the scale you set. A cut list is only as good as the measurements behind it. Do not order material or price a job off RoofMap without checking it against the actual building.

RoofMap is a tool a tradesperson uses to do their own work faster. It is not a survey, not a building report, not engineering advice, and not a substitute for your own judgement or for a site visit. Nothing it produces has been certified by anyone.

The quotes, proposals and job packs you produce are your documents. You decide what goes in them, you set the prices, and the contract for the roofing work is between you and your customer. We are not a party to it. The standard terms and conditions that appear on a proposal are a starting template — read them, change them to suit your business, and get your own advice on them.

The same goes for anything the app works out automatically, including the parts that use AI to read an aerial photo or a supplier price list. Those are suggestions to be checked, not answers.

Prices and material quantities

The price book is yours to set, and what RoofMap does with it is arithmetic: it multiplies your rates by measurements you are responsible for. It does not know what your supplier will charge you today. Supplier prices move, and a rate that was right last month may not be right on this job. Check the figures against a current supplier quote before you commit to a customer, and check quantities against the building before you order.

A new account starts on indicative retail figures rather than anybody's negotiated rates, so that the app is usable on day one and so no real buy rate is ever a default. They are a placeholder to replace with your own, not a price to quote off.

You will see this same warning where it matters rather than only here: it is printed on the material order form, on the cut list, and on the cover of the job pack the crew works off.
