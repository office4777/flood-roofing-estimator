The job pack

The cut list, the flashing schedule and the merchant order, off the same drawing.

Everything the crew needs to lay the roof and everything the merchant needs to price it, counted from the roof you traced rather than typed a second time.

The job pack is the drawing, counted. Sheet lengths and quantities, a flashing schedule listing each piece at its own length, gutter, underlay, fasteners, a dektite and a back-tray for every pipe, and the five-piece set a boxed chimney needs. Every row is editable, and what prints is what gets ordered.

The cut list is the drawing, counted

Nothing in the job pack is typed twice. The sheet lengths are the sheet runs off the drawing, the ridge metres are the ridge lines, the valley metres are the valley lines. Change a measurement on the roof and the list changes with it.

What comes out is what a merchant can price and a crew can lay:

  • Roof sheets — quantity at each length, by profile and gauge
  • Ridging and flashings — ridge, hip, valley, barge, apron, change-of-pitch, each listed as 1 @ 6.8 m rather than a total
  • Gutter and downpipes — with the fittings that go with the profile
  • Underlay, screws and rivets, counted off the actual sheet and flashing metres
  • Pipe flashings — a dektite and a back-tray for every penetration
  • Boxed penetrations — the five-piece set a chimney needs

Lengths, not totals

A flashing line that says "42 m of barge" is not an order. The merchant folds and cuts to length, and 42 metres could be six pieces or fifteen. RoofMap lists each piece — 1 @ 6.8 m, 1 @ 3.7 m — because that is the form the order has to be in, and because it is the form the crew can check against the roof.

Wastage is added per piece rather than as a percentage on the total. A percentage is a guess that is wrong in both directions: generous on a simple roof, short on a cut-up one.

The two things that get left off orders

Back-trays and boxed-penetration flashings are the two items that reliably get found on the roof rather than on the order. Both are automatic here.

Every pipe penetration raises two lines, not one: the dektite for the pipe size, and the back-tray behind it, with its length worked out from the pipe and the sheet profile. A boxed penetration brings a back flashing, two sides, a front apron, and a saddle from the ridge to the top of the box.

The whole job's flashing metres, on screen

Pricing shows one roof at a time, which is right for pricing and dangerous for checking. So the strip above the priced material carries the whole job's flashing metres — every roof added up, shared edges counted once.

If that number reads low against what you paced out on site, a line is missing somewhere. It is the fastest check there is on a take-off, and it is deliberately the one number you cannot get to by looking at a single roof's tab.

It is a designer, not a report

Click any field and edit it. Re-quantify a row, change a length, delete one, add one. Add a page, delete a page, reorder them. Drag any of the roof maps — detailed, basic, the sheet layout, the sheet calculation check — onto a page and it drops in as a movable, resizable picture.

What prints is exactly what is on screen. Save as PDF for the crew; Order Roof sends the material order to the merchant, showing you the cut-list summary in the popup first.

The order follows what the customer chose

If the customer upgraded the grade, the gauge or the gutter on their proposal, that is what the merchant is quoted. The specification on the job pack follows the selections rather than the defaults you started from — which is the gap that used to send an order out as corrugate and 0.40 for a customer who had paid for 5-rib and 0.55.

Questions

What roofers ask about this.

Can I change a quantity or a length by hand?

Yes. Every row in every cut list is editable — quantity, length, description — and rows can be deleted or added. What you change is what prints and what gets ordered.

Manual edits stick. They are not overwritten the next time the drawing changes, because an edit usually means you know something the drawing does not.

How is wastage calculated on flashings?

Per piece rather than as a percentage of the total, and rounded up to the next 0.1 m. A percentage on a total is a guess that runs generous on a simple roof and short on a cut-up one, which is the wrong way round.

Does the job pack include the flashing profiles?

If you have saved them. Settings → Flashing library holds a folded-profile drawing per flashing name, with the crush and painted faces marked and each fold dimensioned. Save one under the exact name a flashing uses and it prints beside that flashing's lengths.

Can I produce a job pack for one roof out of several?

Yes — the Roofs included buttons at the top filter the whole pack, and the cut lists, consumables and merchant order all follow. Useful when the garage is going on next month and you want its order separately.

Can the crew get this on their phone?

Save as PDF and send it, or push it to Fergus if you run it. The pack has the map, the cut lists and the consumables — everything needed to lay the roof without ringing the office.
